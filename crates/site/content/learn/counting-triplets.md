A triplet fits **three notes into the time of two**. The most common one, the eighth-note
triplet, puts three even notes on one beat, where two eighth notes would normally go. It
is written with a small "3" above the notes and counted **"1 trip let"**.

```rhythm bpm=70 | Straight eighths, then eighth-note triplets
4/4 e e e e t8 t8 t8 t8 t8 t8
```

## Triplets with rests

The tricky part is triplets with rests. Keep counting all three syllables and play only
where there is a note:

| Figure | Counted | Played on |
|---|---|---|
| x x x | 1 trip let | all three |
| x – x | 1 – let | 1, let (the shuffle) |
| – – x | – – let | let |
| x x – | 1 trip – | 1, trip |
| – x x | – trip let | trip, let |
| – x – | – trip – | trip |

```rhythm bpm=70 | The shuffle and the "let"
4/4 t8 r:t8 t8 t8 r:t8 t8 r:t8 r:t8 t8 r:t8 r:t8 t8
```

```rhythm bpm=66 | Two notes out of three
4/4 t8 t8 r:t8 r:t8 t8 t8 t8 t8 r:t8 r:t8 t8 t8
```

## Quarter-note triplets

A quarter-note triplet spreads three notes over **two** beats. Count eighth-note triplets
and play on every second syllable: "**1** trip **let** 2 **trip** let".

```rhythm bpm=70 | Quarter-note triplets against the beat
4/4 t4 t4 t4 q q
```

## FAQ

### What is the difference between triplets and swing?

Swing plays written straight eighths with a long-short feel. At 66% swing, a pair of
eighths sounds like the shuffle figure "1 – let". See [swing](/metronome/swing/).

### Are triplets the same as 12/8?

Close. A 4/4 measure of eighth-note triplets sounds like a 12/8 measure of eighths; 12/8
is simply written without the triplet brackets.
