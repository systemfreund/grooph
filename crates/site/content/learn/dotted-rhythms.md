A dot after a note makes it **one and a half times as long**. A dotted quarter lasts a
quarter plus an eighth; a dotted eighth lasts an eighth plus a sixteenth. Dotted notes
almost always come with a short note that fills up the beat.

| Note | Length | Usually paired with |
|---|---|---|
| dotted quarter | 1½ beats | an eighth note |
| dotted eighth | ¾ of a beat | a sixteenth note |
| dotted sixteenth | ⅜ of a beat | a thirty-second note |

## Dotted quarter + eighth

Count in eighths: the dotted quarter takes "1 & 2", the eighth falls on the "&" of 2.

```rhythm bpm=80 | "1 & 2 (&)" – dotted quarter and eighth
4/4 q. e q. e
```

## Dotted eighth + sixteenth

Count in sixteenths: the dotted eighth takes "1 e &", the sixteenth falls on the "a".
Played in a row, it sounds like a galloping horse.

```rhythm bpm=70 | The dotted gallop
4/4 e. s e. s e. s e. s
```

Turned around – sixteenth first – it becomes the "Scotch snap":

```rhythm bpm=70 | Sixteenth + dotted eighth
4/4 s e. s e. s e. s e.
```

## Don't confuse it with swing

A dotted eighth + sixteenth is a 3:1 ratio. [Swing](/metronome/swing/) at 66% is 2:1 and
sounds softer. Play the two one after the other:

```rhythm bpm=80 | Dotted (3:1), then triplet feel (2:1)
4/4 e. s e. s t8 r:t8 t8 t8 r:t8 t8
```

## FAQ

### What does a double dot mean?

A second dot adds half of the first dot again: a double-dotted quarter lasts
1 + ½ + ¼ = 1¾ beats.
