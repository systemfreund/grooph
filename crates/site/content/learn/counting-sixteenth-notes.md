A sixteenth note is a quarter of a beat in 4/4 time. Four of them fill one beat, and they
are counted **"1 e & a"** (say "one ee and uh"). The number falls on the beat, the "&"
exactly halfway, and "e" and "a" in between.

```rhythm bpm=60 | Four sixteenths on every beat
4/4 s s s s s s s s s s s s s s s s
```

## The common sixteenth figures

Almost every sixteenth-note rhythm is built from a handful of one-beat figures. Learn them
as shapes, the way you learn words instead of letters.

| Figure | Counted | Played on |
|---|---|---|
| four sixteenths | 1 e & a | all four |
| eighth + two sixteenths | 1 – & a | 1, &, a |
| two sixteenths + eighth | 1 e & – | 1, e, & |
| sixteenth-eighth-sixteenth | 1 e – a | 1, e, a |
| dotted eighth + sixteenth | 1 – – a | 1, a |
| sixteenth rest + three sixteenths | – e & a | e, &, a |

```rhythm bpm=60 | Eighth + two sixteenths, then two sixteenths + eighth
4/4 e s s e s s s s e s s e
```

```rhythm bpm=60 | Sixteenth-eighth-sixteenth and the dotted "gallop"
4/4 s e s s e s e. s e. s
```

```rhythm bpm=56 | Notes on "e" and "a"
4/4 r:s s s s r:s s e r:s e s r:e. s
```

## Step by step

1. **Count first, then clap.** Say "1 e & a" steadily with the metronome before you play
   anything.
2. **Clap only the notes**, but keep saying every syllable.
3. **Start at 60 BPM** or slower. Sixteenths at 60 BPM are four notes per second.
4. **Mind the rests.** A rest on "1" is the hardest case – say the "1" quietly and come in
   on the "e".

## FAQ

### Why "e" and "a"?

They are simply easy syllables that keep the four notes even. Some teachers use
"1 ta & ta" or "1 ee and ah"; the system matters less than saying it consistently.

### How do I count sixteenths in 6/8?

In 6/8 the eighth note is the beat unit, so a sixteenth is half a beat: "1 & 2 & 3 &".
