Sixteenth notes divide each beat into four, counted **"1 e & a"**. With them come the
figures you see in almost every pop, rock, funk and classical piece: gallops, dotted
rhythms and syncopations on the "e" and the "a". grooph's generator introduces them in
five levels.

## The levels

Each level adds new one-beat figures; easier figures keep appearing.

| Level | New figures |
|---|---|
| 1 | quarter note, quarter rest, two eighths |
| 2 | eighth rest + eighth (note on "&") |
| 3 | four sixteenths, eighth + two sixteenths, two sixteenths + eighth |
| 4 | sixteenth-eighth-sixteenth, dotted eighth + sixteenth, rests inside the beat |
| 5 | figures starting with a sixteenth rest (notes on "e" and "a") |

## Level 3: sixteenths on the beat

```rhythm bpm=70 | Level 3 figures
4/4 s s s s e s s s s e q
```


## Level 4: dotted figures and inner rests

The dotted eighth plus sixteenth is a long-short "gallop", counted "1 – – a". The
sixteenth-eighth-sixteenth figure is short-long-short: "1 e – a".

```rhythm bpm=70 | Level 4 figures
4/4 e. s s e s r:e s s s s r:e
```

```open | Practice level 4
bpm=70&sub=16&lvl=4&bars=2
```

## Level 5: notes on "e" and "a"

Every figure starts with a rest, so the first note comes a sixteenth after the click.
These are the hardest to read – count every syllable.

```rhythm bpm=60 | Level 5 figures
4/4 r:s s e r:s s s s r:s e s r:e. s
```

```open | Practice level 5
bpm=60&sub=16&lvl=5&bars=2
```

## Tips

- **Say all four syllables** ("1 e & a") even when there are rests – especially then.
- **Slow down for level 5.** 60 BPM is fast enough at first.
- **Use ghost notes.** In the generator, switch on ghost notes to hear quiet clicks on
  every sixteenth you don't play.
- Read the [guide to counting sixteenth notes](/learn/counting-sixteenth-notes/) if a
  figure is unclear.

## FAQ

### How do you count a dotted eighth and a sixteenth?

The dotted eighth lasts three sixteenths ("1 e &"), the sixteenth falls on the "a". Say
"1 – – a, 2 – – a".

### What is the difference between level 4 and 5?

Level 4 figures still start on the beat (or with a full eighth rest). Level 5 figures
start with a sixteenth rest, so the first note lands on the "e".
