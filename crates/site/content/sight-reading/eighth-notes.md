Eighth notes divide each beat into two halves, counted **"1 &"**. They are the first
step in rhythm reading, and almost everything later – sixteenths, syncopation, swing –
builds on a solid feel for the "&".

## Level 1: on the beat

Quarter notes, quarter rests and pairs of eighths. Every beat starts with a note or a
rest you can see.

```rhythm bpm=80 | Level 1
4/4 q e e r:q q | e e e e q r:q
```

```open | Practice level 1
bpm=80&sub=8&lvl=1&bars=2
```

## Level 2: the "&" on its own

Now a beat can start with an eighth rest, so the note falls on the "&". Count the rest
out loud ("1 **&**") and play exactly between the clicks.

```rhythm bpm=80 | Level 2
4/4 r:e e q r:e e e e | q r:e e r:e e q
```

```open | Practice level 2
bpm=80&sub=8&lvl=2&bars=2
```

Level 2 already contains every eighth-note figure. For more variety, add **space** in the
generator (more rests) or more bars, then move on to
[sixteenth notes](/sight-reading/sixteenth-notes/).

```open | Level 2 with rests and 4 bars
bpm=80&sub=8&lvl=2&bars=4&space=0.4
```

## How to count eighth notes

Say the beat number on every click and "&" exactly halfway in between: "1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &".
Keep counting during rests. Notes on the "&" are the hardest part – tap your foot on the
beat and play when your foot comes up.

## FAQ

### What tempo should I use?

Start around 70–80 BPM. If you can read four bars in a row without stopping, go up by
5 BPM.

### Why are there no eighth rests on the "&"?

An eighth note followed by an eighth rest sounds the same as one quarter note, so the
generator writes the quarter note instead.
