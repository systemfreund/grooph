An eighth-note triplet squeezes **three** notes into one beat, where two eighths would
normally fit. They are counted **"1 trip let"**. Triplets are everywhere in blues, swing,
shuffles and 12/8 grooves, and the figures with rests inside them are a classic reading
challenge.

## The levels

With the triplet subdivision, each level adds new triplet figures (x = note, – = rest).
Quarter notes and quarter rests appear at every level.

| Level | New figures |
|---|---|
| 1 | x x x – the full triplet |
| 2 | x – x – "1 – let", the shuffle |
| 3 | – – x – only the "let" |
| 4 | x x – and – x x |
| 5 | – x – only the "trip" |

```rhythm bpm=70 | Levels 1 and 2
4/4 t8 t8 t8 t8 r:t8 t8 q t8 r:t8 t8
```

```rhythm bpm=70 | Levels 3 to 5
4/4 r:t8 r:t8 t8 t8 t8 r:t8 r:t8 t8 t8 r:t8 t8 r:t8
```

## Tips

- **Feel the three, not the two.** Before playing, count "1 trip let 2 trip let" out loud
  for a few bars.
- **The shuffle (level 2)** is the triplet with the middle note left out. It is the same
  feel as 66% [swing](/metronome/swing/).
- Read the [guide to counting triplets](/learn/counting-triplets/) for more.

## FAQ

### How do you count triplets?

"1 trip let, 2 trip let" – three even syllables per beat. Some teachers say "1 la li" or
"tri-pl-et"; use whatever keeps the three notes even.

### Are the triplet levels the same in mixed mode?

No. With the mixed subdivision the triplet order is reversed: the offbeat figure
(– x –) appears from level 1, and the full triplet is added at level 5. The sixteenth
figures keep their levels.
